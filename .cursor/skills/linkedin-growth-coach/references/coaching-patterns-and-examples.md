# Coaching patterns and examples (operator methodology)

This file captures **how an intensive LinkedIn operator diagnoses and prescribes**, with **many short examples** distilled from cohort-style calls. Behavior is **generic methodology**, not impersonation. When you generate copy for users, still follow `human-writing-ai-tells.md` (no em dash, no parallel triple chants, no gimmick intros).

---

## 1. Thinking order (what gets checked first)

Use this stack before inventing new tactics.

1. **Next-week test.** If it does not change profile, DMs, or offer clarity in seven days, deprioritize.
2. **Thesis vs packaging.** Idea may be fine; hook, first lines, image, mobile fold, or “ad smell” may be the failure mode.
3. **Positioning vs content.** Mismatched headline and banner, or banner says X while posts never prove X, reads as confusion, not “bad algorithm.”
4. **Pipeline vs vanity.** Impressions without ICP comments or DMs is a distribution or ICP problem, not a trophy.
5. **Proof before self-story.** If fear blocks a tactic, default to **no data yet** → run the smallest experiment instead of debating in your head.
6. **One job per asset.** Reintroduction is not a sales letter; a carousel test must match the winning template, not a new visual language.

---

## 2. Move: force specificity with a stack of questions

**Pattern:** Broad question comes in → you narrow until you can write a headline.

**Example (offer confusion):**  
User describes data product variants and “build in public.” Response pattern: “What is the offer *now* vs what you want to add?” Then: “Why change if renewals are long and clients stay?” Surfaces real problem: **new client acquisition**, not product shape.

**Prescription pattern:** Keep core contract; **add smaller entry offers** (for example quarterly sprint, ninety-day diagnostic) carved from the same delivery.

---

## 3. Move: replace insider language with buyer language

**Pattern:** Insider titles and credentials mean something to peers, not to cold scrollers.

**Example:** “First chief creator officer in advertising” → peers get it; buyers may not. **Rewrite toward outcome:** “Turn employees into your highest-trust distribution channel” / “Reallocate influencer spend to internal creators.”

**Example (headline mismatch):** Banner says “B2B SaaS predictable MRR in 90 days” but headline says “broken marketing.” Coach move: **one through-line**; prefer concrete SaaS pains (churn, onboarding, retention, pipeline) over “impossible to ignore.”

---

## 4. Move: lead magnet = hook + visual + asset (all three)

**Pattern:** Almost any asset *can* work if the **first line hurts** and the **preview looks like a system**.

**Example analysis:** Hook pairs big number pain with payoff (“46,000 tasks → $23M”) so scrollers see **volume + outcome**.

**Example (rename the magnet):** “Liability score” → user means **owner-dependent business** / burnout / single point of failure. Rename toward **business health**, **operator risk**, or **“can the business run without you”** language.

**Featured section:** Short thesis doc (even fifty words) + email capture + CTA to call.

---

## 5. Move: reintroduction post structure

**Pattern:** LinkedIn favors “I am back / here is who I am” when it is **clear, short, and photo-forward**.

**Checklist:**

- Line 1 ties to **LinkedIn tenure** or lurk history (“on LinkedIn X years, almost no posts”).
- **Introduce** name + credibility in plain English.
- **Interests** + what you will post **going forward**.
- **Why follow** (payoff for reader).
- **PS** that drives comments: “Tag creators you think deserve more reach.”

**Anti-pattern:** Long manifesto + embedded pitch in the same post; split into later posts.

**Example fix:** Draft referenced a company name readers do not know in hook; coach says **keep hook broad**, name specifics mid-post.

---

## 6. Move: storytelling hook discipline

**Pattern:** You are the main character; family or secondary characters need a **bridge** so strangers care.

**Weak opening pattern:** “My journey began with my father’s struggle…” → too many unnamed abstractions.

**Stronger pattern:** Time + stakes + outcome in few words, then expand.

**Example direction:** “By 25 we could not afford X. By 29 I built Y. Here is what happened in between.” Then bullets for moves (move country, qualification, first clients).

**Rule:** Maximum meaning per word in line one; photo **close, crisp**, not tiny group shot.

---

## 7. Move: carousel and doc posts — control variables

**Pattern:** If one carousel hits and another dies, **match winning layout** (fonts, density, cover promise) before blaming the topic.

**Example:** Winning slide set: “Why HR still is not taken seriously…” Flop: different visual system + different promise. Coach: rerun **same format** with topic aligned to hook (“why high performers burn out” + “formula for org success”).

**Formatting:** Avoid **all-caps walls** and emoji-heavy slides that read as “blog pasted into carousel”; use numbered steps + sub-bullets for **authority skim**.

---

## 8. Move: image vs idea mismatch

**Pattern:** Stock or generic image + strong personal story = **packaging failed**.

**Example:** Post A: personal photo + transformation → strong. Post B: stock flag visual + EU policy angle → scrollers do not feel a person. Fix: **screenshot of headline** or source article with highlight, plus clear hook.

**Rule:** For speaker brands, **stage photos** beat abstract AI art when the goal is trust.

---

## 9. Move: broad / narrow / niche content calendar

**Pattern:** Niche the **offer**; keep **content** mix.

**Example calendar (four posts):** One employer-safe “main topic,” one **adjacent** (AI + industry), one **story** (you), one **heavy educational** on the wedge you want to own.

**Within a single post:** Broad hook → narrow teaching → niche PS (lead magnet, service line, hand-raiser).

**Example (solopreneur coach):** Public story can be broad (career pivot, identity). PS points to **specific buyer** and next step.

---

## 10. Move: niching fear vs market size

**Pattern:** “Too niche” is often false if **buyers already match** that slice.

**Example:** “Mid-career ex-corporate marketers going solo” → coach: not too small if **clients already look like that**; double down and gather proof.

**Contrast:** Justin Welsh used as **reference path** — start in a slice, widen later as authority compounds.

---

## 11. Move: clarity call vs blueprint framing

**Pattern:** Buyers want **steps and certainty**, not a label that sounds like therapy.

**Example:** “Free clarity call” underperforms when buyers need **readiness diagnosis**, **tools**, **playbook**. Rename toward **blueprint**, **scorecard**, **stage diagnosis**, **ninety-day plan** — still honest, still their words.

**Content pairing:** Educational posts that **map the path**; PS invites coaching or call.

---

## 12. Move: introvert / vulnerability objection

**Pattern:** Fear of alienating non-introverts is **untested**; relatability often **widens** trust.

**Homework pattern:** Minimum **one story post a week**; ideally **three of five** weekly posts **open with a human line**; use **own comments** to add micro-story.

---

## 13. Move: DMs and warming

**Pattern:** Inbound connection from ICP → **soft** open, nurture, then value ping; not instant pitch.

**Example opens:** “What made you want to connect?” / “How is it going at {company}?”

**Pattern:** ICP liked post → **you** can request connection while top-of-mind.

**Anti-pattern:** Doing lead magnet + profile work but **zero** sends; coach reframes as **self-rejection** and assigns **seven DMs in seven days** style quotas.

---

## 14. Move: pivot and “too many businesses”

**Pattern:** Sequential focus beats simultaneous rebrand.

**Example:** Agency after SaaS tool — explain **why** (delivery and accountability > raw content) if needed; otherwise **just start posting the new world** without over-explaining.

**Profile:** Readers who followed the journey tolerate evolution if **through-line** stays visible.

---

## 15. Move: two ICPs (practitioner + coach-the-coach)

**Pattern:** One **main** public lane (in the trenches); **one** weekly post or featured card for second buyer.

**Example:** Executive coaching for leaders + demand from coaches → **most** content helps leaders; **occasional** post on revenue, practice, or “how I built the book of business.”

---

## 16. Move: technical expert speaking to leaders

**Pattern:** **Simple hook**, **numbered steps**, then **technical depth** proves competence.

**Example (RevOps / HubSpot):** Leaders will not do the clicks; they need to **trust** you know the clicks.

**CTA split:** Most posts → **call to engage** (“which step broke for you?”). Heavy asset posts → **one** clear service or magnet CTA.

---

## 17. Move: quote cards and static photos

**Pattern:** Quote templates **fail** without **writer-level** contrast and specificity.

**Structure taught:** Line 1–2 = tension or contrarian; last lines = **feel-good or save-worthy**; image supports **wealth or authority** positioning **honestly** (real stage, real workspace).

**Video vs photo:** For many builders, **static photo** still gets reach faster than long video without existing brand.

---

## 18. Move: scheduling vs engagement

**Pattern:** First **~30 minutes** after publish matter; schedule-and-forget wastes good writing.

**Homework:** Be present to reply; treat comments as **fuel**, not optional.

---

## 19. Move: validated hooks workflow (AI + swipes)

**Pattern:** Generate hook candidates → **borrow structure**, not topic, from saved posts → rewrite for niche → shorten.

**Example chain:** Age timeline hook + referral dependence → rewrite for **clinical / HR / SaaS** pain.

**Voice memo hack:** Dictate brain dump with “do not generate, only capture” → feed into drafting tool.

**Frameworks named in teaching:** Story-lesson-action-you style flow; problem-agitate-solution; attention-interest-desire-action style sequencing. Use **one** framework per post, not all at once.

---

## 20. Move: newsletter vs feed post

**Pattern:** Major visibility moments (stage, keynote) → **feed story** first; newsletter is **follow-up container**, not the main reveal.

**Anti-pattern:** Hiding the best proof inside newsletter-only format.

---

## 21. Move: launch and monetization (distilled blueprint)

Cross-session pattern for **big pushes** (summarized from dedicated launch teaching):

**Phases**

1. **Warm-up (~8 weeks):** ~4 posts/week mixing problem-solving, story, authority proof, and **one** list-building magnet with **strong visual**.
2. **Offer:** Time bound scarcity **or** clear transformation in a timeframe **or** simple “how to get Z in Y days” logic; bonuses pre-handle objections.
3. **Blitz (~4–10 days):** Daily posts + Lives/webinars + **high-volume email**; comments hold proof and links.

**Tactics**

- **Waitlist** on profile during push; email carries most revenue for some operators.
- **Comments** for testimonials, links, extra proof.
- **Packaging:** Visual is the **bait**; clarity beats clever (“$0 test” → “how I made $10k from a digital product” style).

---

## 22. Countable homework patterns (copy these shapes)

Assign numbers, not vibes.

- **Seven DMs in seven days** after profile fixes.
- **One story post / week** minimum; **three of five** posts open with lived detail.
- **Redo carousel** twice more with **graph-led** cover before quitting format.
- **Featured:** workshop booking line + **one** post selling the workshop.
- **Cold email:** five subject lines + **five sample posts** for prospect vertical.
- **Reintroduction:** publish by **fixed day** with **face photo**.

---

## 23. Phrases that signal coach logic (use sparingly)

- “What is the offer in one sentence?”
- “The idea is fine; the **packaging** failed.”
- “You are doing **three jobs** in one post; split it.”
- “No data yet → run the test.”
- “Your peers understood you; **LinkedIn did not.**”
- “Call to **engage** most days; call to **sell** on purpose.”

---

## 24. Anti-patterns this methodology rejects

- Algorithm fatalism before hook and ICP review.
- Explaining the **entire service** in first DM or hero line.
- New carousel layout on every test.
- Lead magnet list with **no** nurture sequence.
- Low-ticket product as a distraction when **high-ticket** sales skill is the real gap.

## Appendix: before / after sketches (teaching shortcuts)

Use these as **direction**, not copy-paste; generated user copy must still pass `human-writing-ai-tells.md`.

| Situation | Weak / vague | Stronger direction |
|-----------|----------------|---------------------|
| Employee advocacy / creator programs | Title stack, insider-only | Outcome: trust, reach, or saved spend in plain English |
| SaaS marketing headline | “Broken marketing” alone | Tie to MRR, churn, onboarding, or pipeline in one line |
| Reintroduction | Long pitch + company jargon in line one | Years on LinkedIn + lurk history → who you are → why follow → PS tag |
| Family origin story | “My journey began with my father’s struggle…” | Age or year + concrete stakes + your outcome in line one |
| HR / ops carousel | New visual style each time | Clone **winning** layout; change **promise** only |
| Policy / news take | Stock image, no human | Screenshot of source + clear stake for reader |
| Clarity offer | “Free clarity call” | Blueprint, readiness score, stage diagnosis, or ninety-day plan language |
| Lead magnet name | Internal metaphor (“liability”) | Buyer words: health, dependency, time off, revenue leak |
| Service dump in DM | Ten deliverables listed | One outcome, one proof, one ask |
| Technical expert | Feature release notes tone | “How to get {outcome} with {tool} in {n} steps” hook, then detail |
| Purpose-led coach | “Find your purpose” as lead | Money, career, or burnout hook; purpose as **phase one** inside delivery |
| Pivot post | Newsletter gate for big win | Feed announcement + photo; link in comment or part two |
| Launch week | Random tips | Problem posts tuning to the offer; waitlist visible; email volume planned |
